# TACO: is membership worth it for a 12-site park?

Researched 2026-11-03 (web only; nothing submitted, nobody contacted). TACO is the Texas
Association of Campground Owners: tacomembers.com (members), texascampgrounds.com (public),
tacoguidemediakit.com (ad sales). Companion to `listing-copy.md` §5, which holds the e-mail.

Each claim is marked **C** (seen on a primary or industry page) or **I** (inferred).

## Short version

- Dues for a park with 0–25 sites were a flat **$460 / $475 / $490** in 2014 / 2015 / 2016
  (C, archived dues packets). Nothing newer is public. At the same ~3 %/yr drift, 2026 is
  likely **$620–$720**, ~10 % less with the early-bird (I). That is the whole cost for us: the
  small band has no per-site charge.
- Small add-ons historically: Direct Link to our own website $75/yr, first-time guide setup
  $40, listing change $20 (C, 2015–16 packets). Ask whether these still apply.
- The membership and guide year run January–December. Invoices go out in July, 10 % off
  "Section A" (dues) if paid by **Sept 1**, and Sept 1 is also the listing-copy deadline for
  the January guide (C). **Joining in November 2026 almost certainly misses the 2027 printed
  guide**; historically late joiners went into a "new member insert," not the bound book
  (C, 2007 join page). The web listing would be immediate (I). First full year of value is 2028.
- What a basic listing is: a line in the printed guide with contact details, amenities and a
  spot on the finder map, plus a full page on texascampgrounds.com (photos, map, amenity
  filters, a Daily / Weekly / **Extended Stay** rates block, and a link to our site) (C).
- The **Extended Stay page** on texascampgrounds.com is **not** a filter every member lands
  on. It is a hand-curated page of ~15 parks statewide that mirrors a printed section, each
  with a page reference into the guide; it looks like a paid or featured add-on (C that it is
  curated, I that it is paid; no price found). None of the 15 are in the Hill Country.
- Reach claims (TACO's own, unverified): 250,000 printed copies a year, 12–14 TxDOT travel
  centres, Houston and Austin RV shows, 25–50 out-of-state shows, Issuu flipbook,
  "500,000+ annual visitors" to the website. Their 2025 reader survey says **8 % of readers
  are full-time RVers**, 46 % take 4+ trips a year, 86 % keep the guide (C as claims).
- texascampgrounds.com did not show up in the top ~20 results for "Bandera RV park monthly
  rates" or "long term RV park Texas Hill Country"; Hipcamp, Campendium, RoverPass, Campspot,
  The Dyrt and park sites did (I, search-engine proxy, not a logged-out Google check).
- No owner testimonial, forum post or case study attributes bookings to the guide or site
  (not found). The media kit's "28 % more out-of-state bookings" is unsourced and about
  display advertisers, not basic members.
- Members voted **legislative and legal work** the top benefit in a 2022 survey (C). For a
  park full of monthly residents, that side is arguably worth more than the listing. See
  below.

**Read:** the listing alone will not bring full-timers; the guide's readers are mostly
travellers and the long-term page is a separate, likely paid, section. Where TACO earns its
dues for us is the legal side: the monthly site agreement forms, the legal hotline, the
Chapter 762 evacuation-plan template, and the liability sign wording. If we join, join for
that and treat the listing as a bonus. If the e-mail comes back at ~$700 all-in, it is a
modest yes; above $1,000 it is a no unless Extended Stay placement is included.

## Legal and legislative side (C unless marked)

This is the part that matters to a park of monthly residents.

- **Tenant or guest?** SB 1268 (2013) removed RVs from Property Code Ch. 94 (manufactured-home
  lots), so Ch. 94 no longer covers RV spaces. Ch. 92 §92.152 exempts transient housing. AG
  Opinion KP-0222 (2018-11-21, brief filed by TACO's lawyer Bill Cobb) says status "depends on
  the terms of the parties' agreement": a monthly agreement worded like a lease can create a
  tenancy that needs a Ch. 24 eviction; otherwise Penal Code 30.05 (trespass) and 31.04
  (theft of service) apply. **The wording of our site agreement decides it.** TACO's
  members-only forms include monthly site agreements, campground rules and theft-of-service
  processing, plus a Legal Handbook and (new 2025–26) a "Texas Campgrounds Legal Hotline".
- **Liability:** HB 2636 (2023) → Civil Practice & Remedies Code Ch. 75B, limited liability
  for inherent camping risks **if the statutory warning sign is posted**. The handbook has
  the sign wording. Worth posting whether or not we join.
- **Chapter 762 (Heaven's 27 Camp Safety Act, SB 1, 89th 2nd called session, signed
  2025-09-05)** → Health & Safety Code Ch. 762: every private campground and RV park must
  comply with NFPA 1194 (2021), adopt an emergency evacuation plan, and cabins in the 100-year
  floodplain need roof ladders; local rules may not exceed NFPA 1194. Applies to existing
  parks. TACO pushed it and supplies a plan template and a webinar. **We sit on the Medina
  River; check whether we have a written evacuation plan** (I that it applies to us; C that
  the statute covers all private RV parks).
- **Taxes:** state hotel occupancy tax treats RV spaces as outside HOT (Comptroller position
  quoted by Llano County) and stays of 30+ days are exempt anyway. RV hook-ups are not
  subject to sales tax (Comptroller STAR 9809838L, 202410030H); RV storage charges are.
  Parks with >50 % long-term residential use can claim the predominant-use **electricity
  sales-tax exemption and a 4-year refund** (nstarba.com). That one may be worth money to us
  regardless of TACO. TACO killed HB 5136 (2023, a county RV occupancy tax) and used
  HB 1612 / SB 595 (2023, end of "phantom reserve" water billing) to fight the City of Olney
  for one park.
- 2027 priorities (Woodall's, 2026-10-14): property tax relief, insurance costs, HOT
  accountability, later school start.

programmes, Suburban Propane, Constant Contact, music licensing since 2025, camp-store and
office supplies), four $500 scholarships, awards (Park of the Year under 125 sites). Spring
Meeting & Trade Show, Conroe, 2027-04-27/28: member $410 early / $485, non-member $495 /
$560, ~350 attendees, 90+ vendors.

## Reputation

Industry press (Woodall's, RVBusiness, Modern Campground) is uniformly positive and largely
press-release driven. No owner criticism found on Reddit, iRV2 or Facebook, and no owner
anywhere states what they paid. Absence of evidence, not endorsement.
