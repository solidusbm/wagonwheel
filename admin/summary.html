What We Built, and Why

Bandera Wagon Wheel RV Park — booking & website engagement

This is a plain-language account of the work behind banderawagonwheelrv.com — not a template site with your name on it, but a real booking system, payment system, and back office built specifically for how this park actually operates. It's organized by what each piece does for the park, not by the order it was built in.

The core booking system

Guests see real availability, not a calendar that has to be checked by hand. Behind that, the database itself — not just the website's code — is set up so that two guests physically cannot book the same site for overlapping dates. Even if something in the app were ever buggy, the database rejects the conflicting booking outright. That's a stronger guarantee than "the app checks first," which is how double-bookings usually happen at other systems.

Pricing is handled by one pricing engine that bills correctly no matter how a site is sold. Some sites rent nightly, some weekly, some only by the month (Site 1, for example, has no nightly or weekly rate at all — it's month-only). A stay is billed using whatever terms that site actually offers, rounding up to the smallest one available. On top of that, every stay is capped at the monthly rate: once a stay is long enough to cost more than a month's rent, it's billed the monthly rate for that month plus the ordinary rate for whatever's left over — never more. That matters because your sites aren't priced identically: nightly, weekly, and monthly rates vary site to site, from $300 a month up to $650 for Site 2, which carries extra amenities. Without the cap, a longer stay could end up costing far more than simply renting the site by the month.

Real Square payment integration

Payments run through Square in production — not a test mode, not a placeholder. Real cards are charged and the money lands in your Square account. Refunds work two ways: the office can refund and cancel a booking directly from the admin panel, and a guest can cancel their own booking from a link in their confirmation email, which refunds them automatically according to the park's cancellation policy.

Card numbers themselves never touch this server. The card entry box on the booking page is rendered by Square itself, inside an isolated frame that only Square can read. That's a deliberate choice for handling payment card data responsibly — it keeps the site out of the business of storing or transmitting card numbers at all.

The guest application system

The online booking form doubles as the park's paper intake application — the same fields the park has always collected on paper (occupants, vehicles, pets, RV information, and so on) are built into the online form. Nothing beyond name and contact details is required to book online; the rest is "pre-filling, not paperwork" — a guest who wants to save time at check-in can fill it in ahead of arrival, and the office finishes whatever's left when they arrive.

Every reservation also has a printable version of its application, built to work as an actual paper form: every field prints whether or not the guest filled it in, with blanks shown as lines to write on. A guest who filled out nothing online still gets a complete blank form handed to them at check-in, exactly like the park's original paper process.

Findability

A handful of pieces work together to help people actually find the park online:

  • Search engine optimization — a written description for each page, a sitemap, and structured data that tells Google directly what the park's rates and amenities are, plus verification set up with Google Search Console so the park can see how it's performing in search.
  • Social sharing previews — when a link to the site is posted on Facebook or texted to someone, it shows a real title, description, and photo instead of a blank box.
  • Automatic review requests — a short email goes out to a guest a day or two after they check out, asking for a Google review. It only starts once the office turns it on and saves a review link, and it goes to every guest with no filtering for happy ones — screening reviewers is against Google's own rules and can get a listing penalized.
  • Social post drafts — a tool that writes ready-to-post copy for Facebook and Google straight from the park's real data: which sites are actually free this weekend, the real rates.

The review requests and social drafts are both built to require a person before anything goes out publicly — nothing posts or sends itself. An automatic "3 sites open this weekend" post can be wrong a minute after it's published, and a person reading it first is the safeguard against that.

Trust and safety

  • Fraud protection — the booking and cancellation pages limit how many attempts can come from one visitor in a short window, aimed specifically at "card testing" (someone using a booking form to check whether stolen card numbers work).
  • Email authentication — SPF and DMARC records are published for the domain, so mail servers everywhere know that nothing but the park's real sender is allowed to send email claiming to be from banderawagonwheelrv.com.
  • A privacy page written to describe only what the site actually does, checked against the real code rather than assumed — no cookies of its own, no advertising trackers, and card details that never reach the server at all.
  • Secure cancellation links — the link in a guest's confirmation email is cryptographically signed, so a reservation code alone (which is short and guessable) is never enough on its own to cancel someone's booking or move money.

Real park data, not placeholders

The site map was rebuilt from the park's actual county-filed engineering plan, corrected against a copy the park marked up by hand — that correction fixed the block's orientation, the road layout, and where Sites 1 and 2 sit relative to the rest. It also caught a real mismatch: the engineering drawing numbers each row in one direction, but the physical markers in the park read the opposite way. The map now matches what a guest standing in the park actually sees, not what the drawing says — a genuinely easy thing to get wrong and send guests to the wrong pad over.

Every rate, amenity, and rig length shown to guests was entered by the park itself, not guessed at, and the photos throughout the site are real photos of the park, not stock imagery.

Design and accessibility

The site uses a light, warm color palette chosen for the park, built directly into the stylesheet rather than swapped in after the page loads. Every text color against every background was measured for contrast, not just eyeballed — a check that caught several genuinely hard-to-read spots (including the order total on the booking page) that looked fine at a glance but failed a real contrast calculation.

The site and admin panel both work properly on a phone: admin tables that would otherwise run off the edge of the screen turn into stacked cards below tablet width, and the homepage's layout was fixed so its artwork and text don't overlap on a smaller screen.

Operational tooling for the park

Beyond the guest-facing site, the office has a full admin panel for running things day to day: managing reservations, editing site details and rates, uploading and ordering photos, editing homepage text, and moving pads on the park map, all without needing a developer or a deploy. It also includes:

  • Sync feeds for RoverPass and Hipcamp, so a booking made directly on the park's own site blocks that site on those platforms too.
  • Push notifications straight to a phone the moment a guest books, on top of the existing email alert.
  • A drag-and-drop editor for the park map itself, so the office can move a pad or relabel a building without asking anyone to touch code.

Where things stand

Development on a site like this is iterative, not a single finish line — this document reflects what's built . A short list of things still genuinely open, none of them urgent:

  • The park map's pad and road sizes are drawn to a uniform placeholder size rather than measured exactly — positions come from the filed plan, but a few paced-off distances at the park (or a careful read of the plan's printed scale) would let the map show true sizes instead.
  • Two settings that live in the park's own Cloudflare account, not in this site's code, were just decided: email addresses will stop being obfuscated from crawlers on the page, so contact links work normally again without needing JavaScript — that still needs someone with access to the park's Cloudflare account to flip the switch. Cloudflare's built-in visitor analytics will stay on; it's cookieless and already disclosed on the privacy page.
